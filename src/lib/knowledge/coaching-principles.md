# Coaching Principles — General Strength Programming

## Core Philosophy

Training is built around **bone density, structural integrity, and multi-planar human movement**. Every program decision runs through the question: does this safely load the athlete to build the stimuli they need, or is it noise?

Athletes train all planes of human movement. We use mechanical tension through long ranges of motion (dumbbells, barbells) and heavy stimulus through short ranges (13" deadlifts, farmer carries). No bosu balls, no balance-board work — only tools that produce real adaptation.

The target athlete is capable of hard work on general strength programs.

---

## What "Athletic" Means

An athlete who has been through this system can:
- Move through all fundamental human movement patterns with limited pain
- Express above-average explosiveness, strength, agility, reaction time, and technical efficiency
- Demonstrate solid base strength in the following lifts:
  - Back squat
  - Front squat
  - Close grip bench press
  - Overhead press / push press
  - Deadlift
  - Trap bar deadlift
  - Rowing movements

---

## Training Methods Used

- Heavy compound lifting
- Plyometrics
- Loaded movement (axial and front-loaded)
- Unilateral movements
- Tempo work
- Pauses in weak positions
- Isometrics for soft tissue and positional strength
- Core rotational work
- Dynamic warmups
- Technical efficiency practice
- Hypertrophy work
- Strongman implements (see below)

## Trusted Exercise Library

### Lower Body
- Below knee (13") deadlifts
- Pause deadlifts (accessory on deadlift days)
- Beltless stiff leg deadlifts
- Trap bar deadlifts
- Hack squats
- Hamstring curls — 2x/week
- Isometrics on leg extensions and leg curls
- Add/abduction machines
- Copenhagens
- GHDs

### Upper Body — Push
- Close grip Spoto bench press
- Neutral grip bench
- DB incline bench — 1x/week minimum (vary tempo, reps, intensity)
- Shoulder pressing — 1x/week
- Tricep accessories — 1x/week

### Upper Body — Pull
- Seated rows with pause
- Underhand lat pulldowns — 2x/week
- Rear delt flyes — high volume
- Bicep work — 2x/week

### Posterior Chain / Hinge
- SSB good mornings
- Beltless stiff leg deadlifts
- GHDs

---

## Progression Model

### Main Lifts (1–2 per session)
- Target: **+5% of 1RM average per week** within a block
- Loading style: linear or undulating — adjust based on how fatigued the athlete is

### Accessory Work
- **Strength / general blocks:** Add 1 set every other week. By end of a 4-week block, each accessory is at +1 set total. Intensity increases and reps decrease as weeks progress.
- **Hypertrophy blocks:** Keep reps higher. Add 1–2 sets per exercise over the block. Layer in pauses or tempo as the block progresses.

---

## Program Structure Principles

- **Block lengths:** 4 weeks (3x/week), 8 weeks (4x/week), 12 weeks (4–5x/week)
- Farmer/frame carries before deadlift sessions as a neural primer
- Hamstring curls appear twice per week across the week's split
- Bicep work appears twice per week
- Rear delt flyes are programmed for high volume (don't skip them)
- DB incline bench appears at least once per week with intentional variation

---

## What to Avoid

- Bosu balls, balance boards, or any "foofy" stability equipment
- Movements with poor ROI for real strength and structural development
- Programming that ignores the athlete's fatigue state (always adjust main lift loading if they're smoked)
